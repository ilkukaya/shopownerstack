Migrations fail in predictable ways. Almost nobody loses their customer list; plenty of
shops lose two weeks of productivity, a month of recurring invoices, or a folder of job
photos they needed for a warranty dispute.

Here is the plan that avoids it.

## Should you switch at all?

Ask what specific decision you cannot make today. "The software is annoying" is not a
reason - every product is annoying by month six. "I cannot see labour cost per job" or "I
cannot tell which adverts produce booked jobs" are reasons, because they name what you are
buying.

If you cannot finish the sentence, stay where you are and spend the effort on your price
book instead.

## Week one: export everything while the old account is live

Take the CSV exports for every object type on the same day so the cross-references still
line up. Then take the things that are not in the CSVs, which is where migrations actually
go wrong:

- **Job photos and signed forms.** Usually downloadable one job at a time. Start early.
- **Automation rules.** Quote follow-up sequences and reminder schedules do not export.
  Screenshot them.
- **Recurring agreements with their next-visit dates.** The dates are what break.
- **Call recordings**, if your system has them.

## Week two: rebuild, do not import, the price book

A straight import carries across two years of drift - prices you meant to update, line items
nobody uses, duplicates. Rebuilding takes an afternoon and is the only chance you will get
to reprice without a customer conversation.

## Weeks three and four: run both systems

New jobs go into the new system. Open jobs finish in the old one. This is not elegant and it
is not optional - one full billing cycle in parallel is what surfaces the things you forgot.

Watch three things specifically: that invoices are reaching customers, that card payments
are settling into the right account, and that the accounting sync is posting to the right
codes. Check the first three invoices line by line with whoever does your books.

## Week five: move the recurring work by hand

Recurring maintenance visits and service agreements almost never import with the correct
next dates. Rebuild them manually and verify the first generated visit for each one.

This is the failure that costs real money, because a recurring visit that silently stops
generating is revenue you do not notice missing for months.

## Week six: redirect and cancel

Update your website booking link, Google Business profile and email signatures. Take one
final full export. Then cancel.

Keep read-only access for a month if the vendor allows it. If they do not, keep the export
somewhere you can search it.

## What are the four things that go wrong?

1. **Job photos** left behind because nobody realised they download one at a time
2. **Recurring visits** that stop generating and are not noticed for a quarter
3. **Accounting mapping** that posts to the wrong codes for a month before the bookkeeper
   catches it
4. **Cancelling too early** - especially with a hosted phone number, where cancelling before
   the port completes can take your main line off the air
