A no-show in an appointment business is not a lost hour. It is a lost hour that you also
staffed, which means it costs more than an empty slot you never sold.

Most salons and studios treat it as a customer-behaviour problem. It is a systems problem,
and four mechanisms fix most of it.

## Mechanism one: card on file at booking

The single biggest change. Not charging the card - just holding it. A client who has entered
card details at booking has made a commitment that a name and phone number does not
represent.

This works even when you never enforce a fee, which is why it is the first thing to turn on.

## Mechanism two: a deposit on high-value services

Set per service, not across the board. A $40 haircut does not need a deposit; a four-hour
colour correction does, and clients booking one expect it.

[Boulevard](/reviews/boulevard/) handles this well - deposits are configurable per service
and the no-show fee captures automatically, which removes the conversation the front desk
would otherwise avoid having.

That avoidance is the real problem. Almost every salon has a no-show policy; very few
enforce it, because enforcing it means a receptionist confronting a regular. Automation is
what makes a policy real.

## Mechanism three: reminder timing, not reminder volume

More reminders do not help. Two, timed properly, do:

- One at 48 hours, which is far enough out that the client can still rebook rather than
  cancel
- One at 2 to 4 hours, which catches the person who forgot this morning

The 48-hour reminder is the one that matters, because it converts a would-be no-show into a
reschedule while you can still fill the slot.

## Mechanism four: an automated waitlist

A cancellation you refill within the hour is not a cancellation. The mechanism has to be
automatic - a receptionist ringing down a list does not happen on a busy day.

In our testing Boulevard's waitlist produced an accepted offer four minutes after a slot
opened up.

## What about class-based businesses?

Different problem. In a studio the equivalent leak is not no-shows, it is failed recurring
payments - members who did not decide to leave, whose card expired and who nobody chased.

Judge fitness software on its dunning first. [Momence](/reviews/momence/) retried three
times over six days and notified the member on the first failure;
[Mindbody](/reviews/mindbody/) needed configuration to do the same.

## What does not work?

Charging fees you do not collect, policies that live only on your website, and asking staff
to have the conversation. All three feel like action and change nothing.

Put the card on file, automate the deposit, set two reminders, and turn on the waitlist. The
rest is noise.
