For a phone-driven trade, an unanswered call is often not a delayed job. It is a job that went to
whoever answered next, because the caller has a problem now and a list of numbers to try.

## How many calls do small service businesses actually miss?

More than most owners think. Published estimates range from about 27 percent of inbound calls for
home services businesses, according to call-analytics firm Invoca,
to 62 percent unanswered by a person in a 411 Locals study of 85 small businesses across 58 industries.

In the 411 Locals study, 37.8 percent of calls were answered live, 37.8 percent went to voicemail
and 24.3 percent got no response at all. The two numbers measure different samples, so treat them
as a range rather than a benchmark. The number that matters is in your own phone provider's call
log. Pull it before estimating anything; owners who guess usually guess low.

## Do callers call back or leave a voicemail?

Mostly not. When Quo (formerly OpenPhone) analyzed 16.7 million missed calls on its platform, only
about 31 percent were returned by the business at all, with the callback rate ranging from roughly
21 to 41 percent by industry.

That is the business side of the problem. The caller side is similar: someone with a leaking pipe
or a locked-out car is not going to sit by the phone. Speed matters for leads generally, too. A
Harvard Business Review study of
online sales leads found that firms responding within an hour were about seven times more likely to
qualify the lead than those that responded later.

## How do you calculate what missed calls are costing you?

Multiply four numbers from your own records:

```
Annual cost = missed calls per week x 52 x booking rate x average job value
```

**Hypothetical example.** Say a locksmith's call log shows 12 missed calls a week. They book about
40 percent of the calls they do answer, and their average job is $185:

```
12 x 52 x 0.40 x 185 = $46,176 a year
```

Those figures are illustrative, not a benchmark. Plug in your own. Two caveats keep the estimate
honest: some missed calls are spam, existing customers or people who will call back, so strip those
out of the count; and on the other side, the formula ignores repeat work and referrals a first job
might have produced.

## What does it cost to answer more calls?

It is the most direct fix and usually the most expensive. For a solo operator that means an
answering service or a receptionist. 2026 pricing guides put many small-business live answering
plans at roughly $135 to $400 a month, with per-minute pricing and overage charges varying widely by
provider (see the Housecall Pro cost guide in the sources below).
Confirm current pricing with any provider you consider.

Before paying for one, ring your own number a few times in a week at different hours and see what
a customer actually experiences.

## Is automatic missed-call text-back worth turning on?

For most phone-driven trades, yes. It is the cheapest fix: a missed call triggers a text within
seconds, offering to book or asking what the caller needs. It works because the caller is often
still deciding who to use.

Many field service products and business phone services offer it. For example, the Workiz help
center documents an automation that texts the caller after a missed call with a link to book.
Note that texts only reach mobile numbers, not landlines. If you do nothing else on this page, turn
this on and watch how many texts get a reply.

## Why track calls by lead source?

Because you cannot manage marketing spend you cannot attribute. Tracking numbers per lead source
tell you which ads produce calls, which calls turn into booked jobs, and therefore what a booked job
costs you.

[Workiz](/reviews/workiz/) builds a phone system with call tracking into its job software. In our
assessment that is its clearest difference from [Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/) and
[Jobber](/reviews/jobber/), which lean on integrations or add-ons for phone features. Check each
vendor's current plan pages, as phone features and add-ons change often. If you buy leads and cannot
state your cost per booked job, you are guessing at your marketing budget.

## Do you need to change software to fix missed calls?

Not necessarily. A standalone business phone service with text-back and call tracking will do most
of it. The argument for having it inside your job software is that the call and the job become the
same record, which makes attribution automatic rather than a spreadsheet exercise.

If your work arrives mainly by referral, repeat contracts or online booking, most of this matters
less and you should not pay a premium for it. Compare

are worth it for your shop.
