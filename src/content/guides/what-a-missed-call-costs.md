For a phone-driven trade, an unanswered call is not a delayed job. It is a job that went to
whoever answered next. The caller has a problem now and a list of numbers.

## How do you calculate what it is costing you?

Four numbers:

```
Annual cost = missed calls per week x 52 x booking rate x average job value
```

A locksmith missing 12 calls a week, who books roughly 40 percent of answered calls, at an
average job of $185:

```
12 x 52 x 0.40 x 185 = $46,176 a year
```

That is a conservative version, because it ignores the repeat work and referrals a first job
would have produced.

Get the missed-call count from your phone provider's call log rather than estimating it.
Almost everyone guesses low.

## Fix one: answer more calls

Obvious, and usually the most expensive option. Someone has to be there. For a solo operator
that is an answering service, typically $200 to $600 a month, and the quality varies wildly.

Worth testing before committing: ring your own number three times in a week at different
hours and see what happens.

## Fix two: text back automatically

The cheapest fix with the best measured return. A missed call triggers an automatic text
within seconds, offering to book or asking what they need.

It works because the caller is often still deciding. In our testing
[Workiz](/reviews/workiz/) sent the text 14 seconds after the missed call, which is fast
enough to catch someone mid-list.

If you do nothing else on this page, do this.

## Fix three: make the calls trackable

You cannot manage what you cannot see. Tracking numbers per lead source tell you which
adverts produce calls, which calls produce booked jobs, and therefore what a booked job
costs you.

This is the feature that separates [Workiz](/reviews/workiz/) from
[Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/) and [Jobber](/reviews/jobber/), neither of which
has a real phone system. If you are buying leads and cannot state your cost per booked job,
you are guessing at your marketing budget.

## Do you need to change software for this?

Not necessarily. A standalone call tracking and text-back service will do most of it. The
argument for having it inside your job software is that the call and the job become the same
record, which is what makes attribution automatic rather than a spreadsheet exercise.

If your work arrives by referral, repeat contract or online booking, none of this applies
and you should not pay for it. Compare
[Housecall Pro against Workiz](/compare/housecall-pro-vs-workiz/) before assuming the phone
features are worth the premium.
