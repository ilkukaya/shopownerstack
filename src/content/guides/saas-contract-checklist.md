Software vendors sell on features and get paid on contracts. The gap between the two is
where small businesses get hurt, and none of it is hidden - it is just not on the pricing
page.

Ask these seven questions on the demo call, before you like the product too much to walk
away.

## 1. Can I export all of my data myself?

The answer you want is yes, self-serve, without contacting support. Ask specifically about
attachments - job photos, signed forms, call recordings - because those are usually the
exception.

In our testing most products export core records fine.
[Mindbody](/reviews/mindbody/) required a support request for a full extract, which is the
kind of friction that matters only on the day you want to leave and have the least leverage.

## 2. How long is the contract, really?

Month-to-month is normal in this market. Annual terms exist and are usually presented as a
discount rather than a commitment. Both are fine as long as you know which you signed.

Pay monthly for the first three months regardless of the discount. You have used the product
for days.

## 3. What is the payment processing rate, and is it negotiable?

Processing is billed separately from the subscription and is often the larger number. A shop
turning over $40,000 a month pays roughly $1,200 in processing at 3 percent - more than any
subscription on this site.

Ask for the rate in writing, ask whether it drops at volume, and ask whether you can use
your own processor. Some products require theirs.

## 4. What happens to my price at renewal?

Ask for the increase cap in writing. "We rarely raise prices" is not a term. A vendor that
will not put a number on it is telling you something.

## 5. What is included in the tier I am being quoted?

Specifically: seats, text messages, job volume, and any third-party subscription the
integration depends on. [Shopmonkey](/reviews/shopmonkey/) integrates with labour guides but
the guide is a separate bill. [Jobber](/reviews/jobber/) caps two-way texting by tier.

Write down the all-in monthly number, not the headline.

## 6. What does onboarding actually include?

How many hours, with whom, and is data import included or extra? A product that needs a week
of setup - [Contractor Foreman](/reviews/contractor-foreman/) does - is a different purchase
from one that is running in half an hour.

If onboarding is charged separately, get the quote before you sign the subscription.

## 7. Who owns the customer relationship?

Ask whether the vendor markets to your customers, whether your customers appear in a
consumer marketplace, and what happens to those relationships if you leave. For most field
service products this is a non-issue. For marketplace-based platforms it is a real question
about whose client the client is.

## The one that matters most

Data export. Everything else is money; that one is leverage. A vendor who makes leaving easy
is confident you will not want to, and that is the most reliable signal on the call.
