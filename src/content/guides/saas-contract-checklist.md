Software vendors sell on features and get paid on contracts. The gap between the two is where small
businesses get hurt, and most of it is not hidden; it is just not on the pricing page. Ask these
seven questions on the demo call, before you like the product too much to walk away. This is
general guidance, not legal advice; read the actual terms before you sign.

## 1. Can I export all of my data myself?

The answer you want is yes, self-serve, without contacting support. Ask specifically about
attachments such as job photos, signed forms and call recordings, because those are often the
exception.

Practices differ. Jobber's help center documents self-serve CSV exports for clients and for invoice
and job reports. Mindbody's support site describes a full subscriber data export that you request
through support, with a questionnaire and a scheduled delivery date. Neither approach is wrong, but a
request-based export adds friction on the day you most want to leave. Our
[Mindbody alternatives](/alternatives/mindbody/) page covers this in more detail.

## 2. How long is the contract, really?

Ask directly whether it is month to month or a fixed term, and what happens if you cancel early.
Annual terms are often presented as a discount rather than a commitment. Both are fine as long as
you know which you signed.

In our assessment, paying monthly for the first few months is the lower-risk choice, even if it
costs a little more. You will have used the product for days, not months.

## 3. What is the payment processing rate, and is it negotiable?

Processing is usually billed separately from the subscription and is often the larger number. For
reference, Square's published US rates are 2.6 percent plus 15 cents in person and 3.3 percent plus
30 cents online on its free plan, and field service products publish their own rates in their help
centers.

**Hypothetical example.** A shop taking $40,000 a month in card payments at a blended 3 percent pays
about $1,200 a month in processing, more than most subscriptions in this category.

Ask for the rate in writing, ask whether it drops at higher volume, and ask whether you can use your
own processor. Some products require theirs.

## 4. What happens to my price at renewal?

Ask for the increase cap in writing. "We rarely raise prices" is not a term. Software prices have
been rising faster than general inflation: spend-management firm Vertice reported SaaS prices up
about 11.4 percent year over year as of January 2025. A vendor that will not put a number on it is
telling you something.

## 5. What is included in the tier I am being quoted?

Ask specifically about users, text messages, job or appointment volume, and any third-party
subscription an integration depends on. Labor guides, parts catalogs, pre-built price books and
phone systems are common examples of things that may be a separate bill or a paid add-on.

Write down the all-in monthly number, not the headline. Our
[pricing explainer](/guides/field-service-software-pricing-explained/) covers the common extras.

## 6. What does onboarding actually include?

Ask how many hours, with whom, and whether data import is included or extra. Products built for
construction project management, such as [Contractor Foreman](/reviews/contractor-foreman/), cover
more ground than a simple scheduling app and usually take longer to set up properly, so budget time
for it.

If onboarding is charged separately, get that quote before you sign the subscription.

## 7. Who owns the customer relationship?

Ask whether the vendor markets to your customers, whether your customers appear in a consumer
marketplace or booking app, and what happens to those relationships if you leave. For most field
service products this is a non-issue. For marketplace-style booking platforms it is a real question
about whose client the client is.

## Which contract term matters most?

Data export. Everything else is money; export is leverage. A vendor that makes leaving easy is
confident you will not want to, which is one of the more useful signals you can get on a demo call.
