The advertised price of field service software tells you less than it seems, because vendors
charge on different bases. A product that looks twice the price can be cheaper at your headcount,
and the other way round. Prices in this market change often, so treat any number here as a snapshot
and confirm on the vendor's site before you buy.

## What are the main pricing models for field service software?

There are four common ones: per user, per block of users, per job and per location. Which one is
cheapest depends on your headcount, job volume and growth plans.

**Per user.** You pay for each person with a login. [Tradify](/reviews/tradify/) prices this way;
its 2026 plans are listed per user per month on Capterra. Simple to understand, and every hire adds
to the bill.

**Per block of users.** A plan includes up to a set number of users, and moving past it means the
next tier or paying for extra seats. [Contractor Foreman](/reviews/contractor-foreman/) plans, for
example, step up by included user count, and [Jobber](/reviews/jobber/) plans also include a set
number of users with extra users charged on top. This is cheapest when your headcount sits just
under a boundary and worst when it sits just over.

**Per job.** [ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/) prices by monthly job allowance, with unlimited users
on paid plans and a per-job charge above the allowance, according to its US pricing page. This can
be the cheapest option when many people need access, and it gets expensive when a busy month runs
past the cap.

**Per location.** Some salon and spa products charge a subscription per location. Third-party 2026
pricing summaries describe [Boulevard](/reviews/boulevard/) this way, with add-ons also charged per
location. Growth in sites, not staff, triggers the bigger bill.

## How do you compare field service software prices fairly?

Work out your own numbers first, then price every candidate against them. You need three:

1. How many people need a login, including the office and the bookkeeper
2. How many jobs you complete in your busiest month, not your average month
3. What headcount you expect in twelve months

**Hypothetical example.** Say a shop has five staff who need logins and does 120 jobs in its busiest
month. A per-user product at $50 a user costs $250 a month. A per-job product with a 150-job tier at
$80 costs $80, with room to spare. A user-block product whose five-user tier costs $150 sits in the
middle, until the sixth hire pushes it to the next tier. These prices are made up to show the
mechanics; plug in the vendors' current published prices for your own comparison.

## What is not included in the headline price?

Four things, routinely:

- **Payment processing.** Billed separately and often larger than the subscription. Square's
  published US rates, for example, are 2.6 percent plus 15 cents in person and 3.3 percent plus 30
  cents online on its free plan. Housecall Pro's help center lists its own card and bank payment
  rates. Ask each vendor for its current rate in writing.
- **Users above the plan allowance.** Easy to miss on user-block pricing.
- **Text messages.** Some products include a text allowance per plan; others charge per message.
- **Add-ons and third-party subscriptions.** Price books, phone systems, marketing tools and parts
  or labor catalogs are often separate bills.

**Hypothetical example.** A shop taking $40,000 a month in card payments at a blended 3 percent pays
about $1,200 a month in processing, which is more than most subscriptions in this category.

## Is annual billing worth it?

It is usually discounted, but it locks you in for a year with software you have used for days or
weeks. In our assessment, paying monthly for the first few months is the lower-risk choice. Once you
are confident the product fits, ask whether you can move to annual billing at the discounted rate.

Also plan for increases. Spend-management firm Vertice reported SaaS prices up about 11.4 percent
year over year as of January 2025, well ahead of general inflation. Ask the vendor what renewal
increases are capped at, and get it in writing. Our
[software contract checklist](/guides/saas-contract-checklist/) covers the other questions to ask.

## When does a cheap product become expensive?

When it stops producing a number you need. A shop that cannot see labor cost per job, or gross profit
per job, can lose more to that blind spot than the difference between a cheaper and a pricier
subscription.

Price the software against the decisions it lets you make, not only against the other quotes. Then
use the [job pricing calculator](/tools/job-pricing-calculator/) to see what an hour of your time is
worth before deciding that saving $60 a month is a win.
