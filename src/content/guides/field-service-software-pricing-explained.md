The advertised price of field service software tells you almost nothing, because the vendors
charge on four different bases. A product that looks twice the price can be half the cost at
your headcount, and the other way round.

## What are the four models?

**Per seat.** You pay for each person with a login. [Tradify](/reviews/tradify/) works this
way on the plans that matter. Simple, and it makes hiring more expensive.

**Per user block.** A plan includes up to a number of users - five, eight, fifteen - and
extra seats cost more on top. [Jobber](/reviews/jobber/) and
[Contractor Foreman](/reviews/contractor-foreman/) work this way. Cheapest when your
headcount sits just under a boundary, worst when it sits just over.

**Per job completed.** [ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/) charges by monthly job volume and
gives unlimited logins. Cheapest by a distance when many people need access, and it breaks
when a busy month trips the cap.

**Per member or provider.** The salon and studio products work this way -
[Momence](/reviews/momence/) by members, [Boulevard](/reviews/boulevard/) by providers. Your
growth triggers the upgrade rather than your hiring.

## How do you actually compare them?

Work out your numbers first, then price. You need three:

1. How many people need a login, including the office and the bookkeeper
2. How many jobs you complete in your busiest month, not your average month
3. What headcount you expect in twelve months

Then price every candidate at all three. A shop with five staff and 120 jobs a month pays
$79 on ServiceM8, $119 on Jobber and $245 on Tradify for broadly comparable work.

## What is not in the headline price?

Four things, routinely:

- **Payment processing.** Usually 2.6 to 3.5 percent plus a fixed fee, billed separately.
- **Extra seats above the tier allowance.** Easy to miss on user-block pricing.
- **Text message overage.** [Jobber](/reviews/jobber/) caps two-way texting by tier.
- **Third-party subscriptions.** [Shopmonkey](/reviews/shopmonkey/) integrates with labour
  guides, but the guide itself is a separate bill.

Add all four before comparing anything.

## Is annual billing worth it?

It saves 10 to 20 percent, and it locks you in for a year with software you have used for
days. Pay monthly for the first three months on any new system. The discount is not worth
being stuck with the wrong product, and any vendor confident in their onboarding will let
you switch to annual later at the same rate.

## When does a cheap product become expensive?

When it stops producing a number you need. A shop that cannot see labour cost per job, or
gross profit per line, is usually losing more to that blindness than the difference between
a $79 and a $249 subscription.

Price the software against the decisions it lets you make, not against the other quotes.
Then use the [job pricing calculator](/tools/job-pricing-calculator/) to see what an hour of
your time is worth before deciding that saving $60 a month is a win.
