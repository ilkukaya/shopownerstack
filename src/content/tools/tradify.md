## What kind of shop is Tradify built for?

Tradify is deliberately narrow. It quotes, it schedules, it captures time and materials
against a job, and it invoices. It does not try to be a marketing platform or a phone
system. For an electrician or plumber whose day already works and who just wants the
paperwork to stop taking evenings, that focus is the selling point.

We ran the Pro plan with three users for five weeks: nine service calls, three quoted
installs and one maintenance contract.

## How fast is quoting?

Fastest here. Saved kits, which are pre-built bundles of parts and labour, turned a
standard consumer unit change into a 1 minute 50 second quote from the phone. If your work
is a small number of job types repeated constantly, this is the mechanism that saves the
most time.

Where it slows down is anything bespoke. A multi-trade job with unusual parts takes about
as long here as it does anywhere else.

## Does the time tracking actually get used?

It is two taps to start and it survives the app being killed, which is the bar most
built-in timers fail. Because the timer is tied to the job, the labour cost on a completed
job is real rather than an estimate somebody typed in later.

That is the number most small trade businesses cannot produce, and it is the main reason to
choose Tradify over [ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/) at a similar price point.

## What is missing?

Everything customer-facing. There are no on-my-way texts, no tracking link, no review
requests and no client portal to speak of. If your complaint is that customers ring the
office asking where the van is, Tradify will not help and
[Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/) will.

Scheduling is also a plain calendar. It is fine for three people and frustrating for ten.

## Is it worth the per-user price?

For a crew of two to five, yes. Past that, per-user pricing compounds and
[Jobber](/reviews/jobber/) becomes cheaper for the same headcount because its plans cover a
block of users. Do the arithmetic at your expected headcount in twelve months, not today's.
