## What problem does Jobber solve?

Jobber is quote-first software. Where Housecall Pro starts with a dispatcher looking at a
board, Jobber starts with a quote going out and then chasing it until it is accepted, done
and paid. If the money you are losing is in quotes that never got followed up, this is the
product that fixes it.

We ran the Connect plan with four seats for six weeks across eleven jobs, deliberately
including three quotes we would normally have let go cold.

## How fast can you actually start using it?

27 minutes from signup to a first invoice in a customer inbox, the quickest of anything we
tested. The onboarding asks the right questions in the right order and the CSV importer
handled a messy 400-row client list without manual cleanup.

Services and products are simpler to set up than a full flat-rate price book, which is part
of why it is faster to start. It is also why quoting a complicated multi-trade job takes
more typing here than in a product built around a price book.

## Does the quoting actually win more work?

This is the strongest part of the product. Optional line items let you put the upsell on
the quote itself and let the customer tick it, which is a better conversion mechanism than
a technician remembering to mention it. Automated follow-up reminders go out on a schedule
you set once.

Across the three quotes we would normally have written off, two came back accepted after
an automated second reminder. That is a sample of three and proves nothing on its own, but
the mechanism is the right one.

## What is weak about Jobber?

Dispatch. The schedule is a calendar with drag-and-drop, not a board built for someone
juggling eight technicians before 8am. If your morning is a dispatcher reshuffling work,
you will be happier on [Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/).

There is also no phone system. Missed calls are the most expensive thing that happens to a
small trade business, and Jobber does not address them. [Workiz](/reviews/workiz/) does, and
that is most of the reason to look at it.

## Who is Jobber right for?

A two-to-fifteen person crew doing residential and light commercial work, where quoting is
the bottleneck and the owner is still involved in every job. Below that,
[ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/) is cheaper for a genuine one-person operation. Above it,
you will start wanting reporting Jobber does not have.
