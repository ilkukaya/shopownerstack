## Is Jobber worth it for a small service business?

For most 1-10 person residential service businesses, yes. Jobber is easy to learn, handles the quote, job, invoice and payment cycle well, and user reviews on G2 and Capterra (both around 4.6 out of 5 in 2026) mostly praise that simplicity.

The case weakens as you grow. Jobber's own pricing now scales with the number of users on each tier, and third-party pricing trackers report increases during 2026. A team of five to ten people can pay several times the headline "from $29" price once seats and add-ons are counted. Prices change often, so check the current numbers on Jobber's site before you commit.

## How much does Jobber cost in 2026?

Jobber lists four plans: Core from $29 a month billed annually (one user, $49 month to month), Connect from $99, Grow from $149 and Plus from $399, each billed annually. Extra users cost about $29 a month each.

Those "from" prices assume the smallest team on each tier. Pricing trackers such as MyQuoteIQ and BaaDigi report five-user and ten-user team pricing well above the single-user figure. The AI Receptionist is listed in Jobber's help centre as an add-on below Plus, and reviews of 2026 pricing describe marketing and pipeline tools as paid extras too. If you want those, compare the add-on total with the Plus price before choosing.

## What does Jobber do well?

Quoting and getting paid. Jobber is built around a quote going out, being followed up, turning into a job and ending in an invoice. On the higher tiers you can add optional line items so the customer picks the upgrade, and automated follow-ups chase quotes that have not been answered.

The client hub is the other strength. Customers get one link where they can approve quotes, see upcoming visits and pay. Online booking and automated reminders are available from Connect upwards. Users frequently mention in reviews that this cuts down on "when are you coming?" calls.

## What are the common complaints about Jobber?

The complaint that shows up most across review sites is the QuickBooks integration: users report sync errors and the need to reconcile by hand from time to time. If your bookkeeper lives in QuickBooks, test the sync during the trial with real data.

Other recurring themes in public reviews:

- **Reporting is basic.** Growing shops say they hit the ceiling on job profitability and technician performance reports.
- **Scheduling is a calendar.** It works for a few crews but is not a dispatch board for a dispatcher juggling many technicians.
- **Billing disputes.** A number of 2025-2026 reviews describe difficulty getting refunds after cancelling, so check the terms before you pay annually.

## How does Jobber compare with Housecall Pro and Workiz?

Jobber is the simpler, quote-first option. [Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/) leans more toward dispatch and marketing tools, and [Workiz](/reviews/workiz/) is built around a phone system with call tracking. See [Housecall Pro vs Jobber](/compare/housecall-pro-vs-jobber/) and [Jobber vs Workiz](/compare/jobber-vs-workiz/) for side-by-side detail.

For a genuine one-person business that mostly does quick jobs, [ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/) can be cheaper because it prices by job volume, not by user. See [Jobber vs ServiceM8](/compare/jobber-vs-servicem8/).

## Who should choose Jobber?

A residential or recurring-service business with one to about ten people, where the owner still quotes most jobs and wants follow-ups and payments to run without effort. Lawn care, cleaning, handyman and small plumbing or electrical shops fit this profile well.
