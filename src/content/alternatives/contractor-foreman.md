## Why do businesses leave Contractor Foreman?

Common complaints in public reviews are a steep learning curve, an interface that feels overwhelming, a mobile app that does less than the web version and scheduling that feels cumbersome for day-to-day service work. Some reviewers also report sync and stability problems.

Contractor Foreman packs in a lot for the price: Gantt schedules, daily logs, change orders, bids, time cards and job costing. For a remodeler or general contractor, that is the point. For a service business, most of it goes unused.

## What is the best alternative for service work?

[Jobber](/reviews/jobber/) or [Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/). Both are built for short jobs: scheduling, dispatch, quoting, invoicing and payments, with customer texts and online booking. Jobber is stronger on quoting, Housecall Pro on dispatch and marketing. See [Housecall Pro vs Jobber](/compare/housecall-pro-vs-jobber/).

## What is a lighter option for a small crew?

[Tradify](/reviews/tradify/) keeps things simple, runs on iOS and Android and includes timesheets and job costing from its Pro plan. It charges per user. See [Tradify vs Contractor Foreman](/compare/tradify-vs-contractor-foreman/).

[ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/) is another option for iPhone crews, priced by jobs per month with unlimited staff.

## Should I switch or fix my setup?

If your work really is projects with phases and change orders, a service tool will not replace Contractor Foreman's project features. Many of the complaints are about setup and adoption, so it can be cheaper to finish setup and train the crew than to migrate.

Prices change, so confirm current plans on each vendor's site.
