## Why do shops leave Contractor Foreman?

Nine times out of ten the setup never got finished. The product needs about a week of
part-time configuration - cost codes, estimate templates, a populated cost database - before
it does anything you cannot get cheaper elsewhere. Shops that skipped that end up with a
dense, slow invoice generator and reasonably conclude it was a mistake.

The other reason is a genuine mismatch. Contractor Foreman is built for projects. Used for
same-day service calls it slows down every job.

## If your work is service, what should you use?

[Jobber](/reviews/jobber/) if quoting is where you win work, or
[Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/) if dispatch and customer communication are the job.
Both will feel dramatically faster, because they are doing far less.

[Tradify](/reviews/tradify/) is the option if you still want real labour cost per job
without the project machinery.

## If your work really is projects, should you switch?

Probably not. Nothing at this price does change orders with e-signature, committed cost
against budget, and live margin on a running job. Switching to a service product means
losing those and managing them in a spreadsheet.

The better move is usually to finish the setup - block two half-days, get the cost codes and
estimate templates right, and reassess after one full project.

## What if the crew simply will not use it?

That is a real constraint and worth respecting. A product nobody opens produces no data, and
cost control built on data nobody enters is fiction. If the crew has rejected it, move to
[Jobber](/reviews/jobber/) or [ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/) and keep project cost control
in a spreadsheet the office owns.
