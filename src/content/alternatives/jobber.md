## Why do shops leave Jobber?

Two reasons, and price is not usually one of them.

The first is dispatch. Jobber's schedule is a drag-and-drop calendar, not a board built for
someone moving eight technicians around before 8am. Shops that grow into that need
[Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/).

The second is the phone. Jobber does nothing about missed calls, and for phone-driven trades
that is the most expensive thing that happens all week.

## What is the direct upgrade?

[Housecall Pro](/reviews/housecall-pro/). Better dispatch, better customer communication,
consumer financing, review requests. You pay more per seat and the quoting is weaker, so
make sure quoting is not what has been winning you work.

## What if the phone is the issue?

[Workiz](/reviews/workiz/). Screen pops with caller history, call recordings, missed-call
text-back in 14 seconds, and tracking numbers that attribute booked revenue to an advert.
Nothing else in this category has it.

Expect the number port to take two to three weeks.

## What if you need to spend less?

[ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/) if you are one to three vans on iPhones -
it charges for jobs rather than seats. [Tradify](/reviews/tradify/) if you want real labour
hours against jobs and quote from a repeatable parts list. Both are narrower than Jobber;
be clear about what you are giving up.

## Is there a reason to stay?

If quoting is how you win work, yes. Optional add-on line items and automated follow-ups are
the best in this category, and none of the alternatives replace them.
