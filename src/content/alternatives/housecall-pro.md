## Why do shops leave Housecall Pro?

Almost always price, and specifically price per seat. The product is good and most people
who leave say so. What pushes them out is a bill that grows every time they hire, plus the
discovery that job costing needs the top tier.

The second reason is a change in the work. A shop that starts doing commercial jobs billed
in stages finds the invoicing model does not fit, and no amount of workaround fixes it.

## What is the closest replacement?

[Jobber](/reviews/jobber/). Same shape, same kind of business, meaningfully cheaper as you
grow because its plans cover a block of users. You lose the dispatch board, which is
Housecall Pro's best feature, and you gain much better quoting.

If the board is what you are keeping the subscription for, be honest about that before you
move.

## What if the problem is purely cost?

[ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/), which charges for jobs completed rather than seats and
gives unlimited logins. For a one to three van shop it is usually half the price or better.
The interface is a generation behind and the Android app is weak; those are the trade-offs.

## What if the work has changed?

If you are now running multi-week projects with change orders and draw schedules, none of
the service-focused products will fit and
[Contractor Foreman](/reviews/contractor-foreman/) is the honest answer. Budget a week for
setup.

## And if phones are the real problem?

[Workiz](/reviews/workiz/). It is the only product here with a genuine phone system, screen
pops and call attribution. It is a lateral move on everything else.
