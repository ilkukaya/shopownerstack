## Why do businesses leave Housecall Pro?

Cost is the main reason. Common complaints in public reviews are add-ons that push the real bill well above the plan price, a price jump once the team passes five users and limited phone support on lower plans.

Housecall Pro still has some of the highest average ratings in the category, with users praising ease of use and customer communication. The complaints are mostly about what it costs to get everything you want, including extras such as vehicle GPS and AI call answering that are sold separately.

## What is the closest alternative to Housecall Pro?

[Jobber](/reviews/jobber/). It covers the same ground for home service crews, has a cheaper entry plan for a solo operator and stronger quoting tools, including optional line items and automated follow-ups.

Jobber's own common complaint is reporting depth, so if reporting is why you are leaving, ask for a demo of the exact reports you need. See [Housecall Pro vs Jobber](/compare/housecall-pro-vs-jobber/).

## What is the cheapest alternative for a small crew?

[ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/), if your crew uses iPhones. It prices by jobs per month with unlimited staff, so a small team with moderate volume can pay much less than on per-user plans.

The trade-off is a limited Android app and fewer marketing features. See [Jobber vs ServiceM8](/compare/jobber-vs-servicem8/).

## What if phones are central to my business?

[Workiz](/reviews/workiz/) builds its product around a phone system with call recording and AI answering. Its pricing is quote-based, so compare written totals. See [Housecall Pro vs Workiz](/compare/housecall-pro-vs-workiz/).

## What if my work is really projects?

If you do remodels or construction jobs with phases and change orders, [Contractor Foreman](/reviews/contractor-foreman/) is built for that, with Gantt schedules, daily logs and project job costing. Expect a steeper learning curve.

Prices and plans change often, so confirm current pricing on each vendor's site.
