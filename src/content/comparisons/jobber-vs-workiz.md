## What separates them?

Where the work comes from.

Jobber assumes you send a quote and chase it. Workiz assumes the phone rings and someone
has to answer it well. Everything else follows from that.

## Is Workiz worth the difference?

Only if the phone is genuinely your acquisition channel. The premium buys three things you
cannot get from Jobber at any price: a screen pop with the caller's history, call recordings
for training, and tracking numbers that attribute booked revenue back to an advert.

If you are spending money on lead generation and cannot say what a booked job costs you,
that attribution is worth more than the price gap. If your work comes from referrals and
repeat customers, you are paying for a feature nobody will open.

## Which is better at the day job?

Jobber, on balance. Faster to start - 27 minutes to first invoice against 46 - better
quoting, better online booking, and a cleaner client portal. Workiz's invoicing works but
is plainer, and its estimates do nothing to help you sell.

The exception is scheduling, where Workiz's dispatch board beats Jobber's calendar if you
are moving several technicians around every morning.

## What about porting your number?

Budget two to three weeks. Ours took eleven days and needed two follow-ups. Keep the old
line live until it completes, and do not schedule the switch during your busy season.

## Recommendation

Default to [Jobber](/reviews/jobber/). Choose [Workiz](/reviews/workiz/) when you can name
the adverts you are buying and cannot say which ones produced booked jobs.
