## Are these even alternatives?

Only in the sense that both are bought by people who describe themselves as tradespeople.
The work is different and so are the products.

Tradify assumes a job starts and finishes inside a day or two. Contractor Foreman assumes a
job runs for weeks, has a budget, and gets billed against a schedule.

Pick the one that matches your work and the comparison is over.

## How much slower is Contractor Foreman to start?

Substantially. 1 hour 48 minutes to a first invoice against Tradify's 31 minutes, and that
understates it. A usable Contractor Foreman setup - cost codes, estimate templates, a
populated cost database - took several days of part-time work on top.

If nobody in the business has that week, do not buy it. It ends up used as an expensive
invoice generator and delivers none of the value.

## What does that week buy you?

Cost control most small contractors do not have. An estimate that becomes a budget, purchase
orders and subcontractor commitments counted as committed cost, and a live margin while the
job is still running.

Plus change orders as a proper signed object. Unsigned scope creep is the most common way a
small builder loses a job's profit, and making the signed change order the easy path is
worth more than most feature lists.

## Where is Tradify clearly better?

Speed of everything. Quoting from a saved kit took 1 minute 50 seconds against 12 and a half
minutes from a cost database. For a shop doing six jobs a day that difference is the whole
argument.

Tradify is also the easiest product on this site to teach to a new hire.

## What if you do both kinds of work?

Most shops that do both are majority one or the other. Buy for the majority. If it is
genuinely fifty-fifty, run Tradify for service work and accept that project cost control
happens in a spreadsheet, which is cheaper than making everyone use
[Contractor Foreman](/reviews/contractor-foreman/) for a two-hour callout.
