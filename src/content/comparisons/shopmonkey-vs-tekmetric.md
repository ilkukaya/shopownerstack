## What is the honest split?

Shopmonkey is a sales tool. Tekmetric is a management tool.

Shopmonkey's digital vehicle inspection, sent to the customer's phone with line-by-line
approval, changes how much recommended work gets authorised. Tekmetric's live gross profit
per line and configurable parts matrix change how much of that work you keep.

Both matter. Which one matters more depends on which number is worse in your shop right now.

## Which is cheaper?

Tekmetric, and the gap is bigger than the headline. Every Tekmetric plan includes unlimited
users; Shopmonkey charges by user blocks. For a shop with three advisors, five technicians
and a bookkeeper, that is the difference between everyone having a login and people sharing
one.

## Is the parts matrix really that important?

For most shops, yes. It sets selling price as a function of part cost, in bands, so cheap
parts get a higher markup automatically. Fourteen minutes to configure six bands.

Shops applying markup by habit are usually losing several points of gross on low-cost parts
without noticing. That is the fastest money either of these products will find you, and
Shopmonkey's markup rules are not as controllable.

## Where does Shopmonkey clearly win?

The customer conversation. Photos of the worn component next to the line item, approvable
individually from a phone, turned outright declines into partial approvals in our test far
more often than a phone call did. The interface is also simply nicer to work in all day.

## What is weak in both?

Scheduling. Shopmonkey's is adequate, Tekmetric's is its weakest module, and neither is a
reason to buy. Most shops run the board loosely regardless.

## Recommendation

Approval problem: [Shopmonkey](/reviews/shopmonkey/). Margin problem:
[Tekmetric](/reviews/tekmetric/). If both are broken, fix margin first - it is the faster
payback and it does not depend on changing how your advisors talk to customers.
