## What is the real difference?

How they charge. ServiceM8 charges for jobs completed and gives you unlimited staff logins.
Jobber charges by a tier that includes a block of users.

For a shop where five or six people need access but the job count is modest, ServiceM8 is
substantially cheaper. For a shop with high job volume and few people, the arithmetic can
flip, and it flips hard if you trip a job cap mid-month.

Work out both numbers with your own headcount and monthly job count before deciding
anything else.

## Which app will your crew actually use?

ServiceM8 on iPhone, comfortably. It holds the full job record locally, so notes, photos,
forms and signatures all work with no signal. We ran a complete job in airplane mode and
nothing was lost.

On Android that advantage disappears. The ServiceM8 Android app trails the iOS one enough
that it changes the recommendation, and Jobber becomes the better choice.

## Where does Jobber pull ahead?

Quoting and everything customer-facing. Optional add-on line items, automated quote
follow-ups, a client hub, real online booking. ServiceM8 quotes fine but does nothing to
chase the quote afterwards.

Jobber is also much easier to hand to a new office hire. ServiceM8's interface is a
generation behind and needs someone to explain it.

## The short version

One to three vans, iPhones, cost-sensitive: [ServiceM8](/reviews/servicem8/).
Three or more vans, Android, or quoting is the bottleneck: [Jobber](/reviews/jobber/).
